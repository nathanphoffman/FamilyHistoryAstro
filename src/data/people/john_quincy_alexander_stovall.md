## Biography

John Quincy Alexander Stovall lived ninety years and spent the back half of them
away from the family that still carries his name. Born in the North Carolina
mountains in 1839, he married Mary Lester in Washington County, Virginia, in 1863,
in the middle of the war. Then, around 1878 or 1880, he left — walked out on Mary
and the children — and went on to marry twice more elsewhere.

His leaving reaches into a later chapter by accident. When his granddaughter Alice
was born in 1886 with no father named, he was already six years and more gone from
the county — yet decades afterward, someone reporting Alice's death seems to have
reached for the only Stovall man they could name and written him into the father's
place. The absent grandfather got pulled back into a story he had nothing to do
with.

## Research Notes

John Quincy Alexander Stovall (1839–1929) married Mary E. Lester in Washington
County, Virginia, on 24 September 1863.[^wash_co_va_marriage_1863] The register
gives his age as 24 (consistent with an 1839 birth), his occupation as
"Carpenter," and his birthplace as "Wilks" — Wilkes Co., N.C., matching the
family's own account. It names his parents as James Stovall and Ann
Stovall.[^wash_co_va_marriage_1863] That conflicts with the family's Ancestry
tree, which gives his parents as Archelaus "Arch" Stovall and Mary T.
Sandifer;[^ancestry_tree_35916700] the primary marriage record is preferred over
the unsourced tree entry.

He abandoned the family around 1878–80 and remarried twice
thereafter.[^jqa_stovall_absence] That absence matters beyond his own story: it
rules him out as the father of his granddaughter Alice, born in 1886, which is
what an informant on her 1965 death certificate appears to have
supposed.[^stovall_death_cert_1965]

## Civil War service

Before the marriage, he had already gone to war. A Quincy A. Stovall enlisted as
a private at Elamsville, Virginia, on 14 June 1861 and mustered into Company D,
51st Virginia Infantry.[^csa_51st_va_stovall] He was captured on 16 July 1864 in
Loudoun County — the record's "London County" is a misreading — and held as a
prisoner of war at Elmira, New York, from 25 July 1864 until his release on 29
May 1865, surviving the war.[^csa_51st_va_stovall] The record gives his
residence as Glade Spring, Washington Co., Virginia, which is what ties this
soldier to our man rather than to a same-named lookalike (below). The timeline
fits cleanly around the marriage: he enlisted in 1861, married Mary in September
1863 (presumably on furlough or between postings), and was captured the
following summer.

## A lookalike, partly

Searching for the records behind his post-1878 departure and remarriages turned
up an outside Ancestry member tree carrying a "John Quincy Alexander Stovall"
with the same birth year (1839) and the exact same death date (9 Jan
1929).[^ancestry_tree_stovall_patrick_co] Most of that profile conflicts with
what the primary marriage record establishes: it gives his birthplace as Patrick
Co., Virginia rather than Wilkes Co., N.C.; his parents as Archelaus Hughes
Stovall and Mary Tabb Sandifer rather than James and Ann Stovall; and a wife,
Rebecca Williams, bearing five children between 1869 and 1879 — years when our
man was still married to Mary E. Lester. Mary does not appear on that profile at
all. That part reads as a different, same-named Stovall from a nearby branch of
the same clan, merged in by mistake.

Its attached Confederate POW card, though, turned out to be genuine — the same
capture, camp and release dates check out independently against
csa_51st_va_stovall, whose residence field (Glade Spring, Washington Co.)
belongs to our man specifically. So the tree is a mixed bag: right about the
soldier, wrong about the father, the birthplace and the wife.
