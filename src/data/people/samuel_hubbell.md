## Biography

On a branch full of names with no lives attached to them, Samuel Hubbell is the
exception — the one who reads like an actual person. Born in 1657 in colonial
Connecticut, he lost his first wife, Elizabeth, to childbirth in 1688, and within
months married Temperance Nichols, herself a widow of four years, taking on a
household with a newborn in it. That is not a scandal or a paperwork slip, as it
has sometimes been treated; it is simply what survival looked like in 1688, and it
is one of the few genuinely human moments this stretch of the tree offers.

## Research Notes

Samuel Hubbell is given by the family's Ancestry tree as standing in the Hubbell
line in colonial Connecticut, a son of Richard Hubbell Sr. and Elizabeth
Meigs.[^ancestry_tree_35916700] The tree states his own descendants loosely — as
the line "reaching back" to him — rather than naming him outright as David's
father, and he is entered in that slot here because the dates fit it, not because
the tree is explicit.

His first wife was Elizabeth Wilson, who died in childbirth in 1688; he married
Temperance Nichols, herself the widow of Jehiel Preston, in April of that same
year. That has been flagged as a chronological problem, and it is not one. A
widower left with a newborn remarrying within months was ordinary in 1688, and
Temperance had been widowed four years by then. It is one of the few things on
this branch that reads as a real life rather than as a bad merge.
