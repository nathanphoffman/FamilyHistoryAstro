## Biography

Nearly every story in this family history reached the present through Colleen
Wallick. The eldest child of Urie John Wallick and Lorene Lewis, born in the
summer of 1935, she was the one who kept the old family Bible and its loose
transcription pages, and the one who carried the spoken memory forward — including
the account of Native American ancestry, which she held firmly had come down her
mother's *father's* side of the house: the Lewis–Sams line, and specifically her
great-grandmother Harriet Eliza Sams.

For a time this history pinned that conviction on the wrong person. Her grandson
Nate recalled her saying only that it ran through "her grandmother", and took that
to mean Colleen's own maternal grandmother, Alice Louise Stovall — which is why so
many early pages went to Alice. Colleen's account, and the paperwork kept with the
Bible, place it up Lorene's paternal line instead; her granddaughter Holly recalls
the same, that Colleen believed it came through her grandmother on Lorene's
father's side, which is Harriet. Whether the tradition ever proves out, it is
Colleen's telling of it that gave the generations after her something to go
looking for.

## Research Notes

Colleen Wallick was the eldest child of Urie John Wallick and Lorene E. Lewis.
The family's Ancestry tree gives her birth as 30 July 1935 at Dover,
Pennsylvania, narrowing what had been recorded here only as 1935 or
1936.[^ancestry_tree_35916700]

The 1950 census supports it, within limits. It records her as Colleen A.
Wallick, daughter, aged 14 in April 1950, born in Pennsylvania — which puts her
birth between April 1935 and April 1936, exactly where the tree puts
it.[^census_1950_york] What the census cannot do is confirm the day: 30 July, and
Dover as the place, still rest on the tree alone.

She is the source of the family's account of its Native American ancestry, and
was firm that it came down her mother's *paternal* side — the Lewis–Sams line
running up through Lorene's father, James Hiram Lewis, to his mother Harriet
Eliza Sams. The handwritten notes on the old family Bible, which came down in
Colleen's keeping and are thought to be partly in her own hand, carry the same
"Cherokee or Creek" tradition on that side.[^bible_notes_lewis]

An earlier reading here placed the account on Colleen's *maternal* grandmother,
Alice Louise Stovall, which is why much of the early work went to Alice's line.
That came from her grandson Nate misremembering her words — she had said the
ancestry ran through "her grandmother", and he read that as her maternal
grandmother. Colleen's own telling, and Holly's recollection of it, both put it
on Lorene's paternal side, i.e. Harriet. The conclusions drawn about Alice's
parentage stand on their own evidence and are unaffected; only the provenance of
the family story has been corrected.
