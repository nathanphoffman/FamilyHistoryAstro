## Biography

George W. Lewis came into the world in December 1860, in Washington County,
Virginia, on the eve of the Civil War. Loss found him early — his father died
while he was still a small boy, and his mother remarried when he was ten. He grew
up in a houseful of sisters who carried unusual, map-drawn names: Virginia,
America, and Missouri.

In the spring of 1882 he married Harriet Eliza Sams. George turned out to be the
family's record-keeper: in an old family Bible he wrote down births and marriages
in his own hand, his wife's name among them, spelled the way he said it aloud —
"Harriet Boar." He and Harriet raised at least six children to adulthood, three
sons and three daughters.

Harriet died in 1913; George lived on another thirty-one years. In old age he
followed his grown children north to the farm country of southern Pennsylvania,
and it was there, on a Friday afternoon in January 1944, that he died at
eighty-three — in the home of his daughter Betty and her husband, where he had
been living. The men who carried his coffin and the women who carried the flowers
were nearly all his own: sons, sons-in-law, grandchildren. He was buried at Fawn
Grove, and the local paper spelled half the family's names by ear.

## Research Notes

George W. Lewis was born in December 1860 and died in 1944, living to
eighty-three — an unusually long life for his cohort. His father died when he was
a small child, and his mother remarried in 1871.[^marriage_1871_kidd_rouse]

He married Harriet Eliza Sams in the spring of 1882 in Washington County,
Virginia.[^wash_co_va_marriage_1882] No occupation has been recorded for him. His
son James's 1905 marriage record names his father only as "G
Wm",[^wash_co_va_marriage_1905] which is the basis for reading his middle name as
William.

## His sisters

The family's Ancestry tree, which attaches no record, gives George three sisters
in his mother's household: Virginia Lewis, born about 1860 and near George's own
age; America Lewis, born about 1864; and Missouri Lewis, born about
1867.[^ancestry_tree_35916700] All three carry place names, a pattern the tree's
compiler singled out as distinctive, and the tree associates the group with a
widowed Margaret Lewis keeping house in Washington County, Virginia, in
1870.[^ancestry_tree_35916700] Nothing is recorded of what became of Virginia,
America or Missouri as adults; that branch is unworked.[^ancestry_tree_35916700]

## The 1944 obituaries

Both notices have now been read from the clippings themselves.

The first, on 8 January 1944, reports that he "died yesterday at 2:30 p. m. at
the home of his son-in-law and daughter, Mr. and Mrs. Baldwin Barbra, this
place," of a complication of diseases, aged 83.[^lewis_obit_1944_death] The
second, three days later, says he died "Friday."[^lewis_obit_1944_burial] Both
point to the same day, and 7 January 1944 was a Friday, so the date is fixed by
the documents rather than reasoned out from a publication date, as it had to be
before the images were in hand.

The first names three sons — James H. of Maryland Line, Walter A. of Knoxville,
Tennessee, and Frank T. of Pylesville, Maryland — and three daughters: Mrs.
James Wyingear and Mrs. Eli Freedman, both of Fawn Grove R. D., and Mrs. Baldwin
Barbra, with whom he lived. Read against James's 1955 obituary, which lists his
siblings as Mary Wyingler, Maggie Freeman and Betty Barbra, the three daughters
resolve to Mary, Maggie and Betty, and the 1944 notice supplies their husbands'
names.

The second gives the funeral: services at the W. Howard Webb Funeral Home in
Fawn Grove, Rev. A. E. Ackerman of the Fawn Grove Methodist church officiating,
interment at Fawn Grove Cemetery.[^lewis_obit_1944_burial] The pallbearers were
Harry Hovis, Lewis Walker, William Barbra, George Barbra, Robert Wynigear and
Donald Lewis; the flowerbearers Ruth Freeman, Emma Freeman, Mabel Freeman, Mrs.
Lewis Walker, Cathrine Kunkle and Gladys Wynigear.

That list is a family gathering written down. Hovis, Kunkle, Barbra, Wynigear and
Donald Lewis all reappear eleven years later in James's own obituary among his
children and their spouses. The surnames are spelled loosely across the two
notices — Wyingear and Wynigear, Freedman and Freeman — which is ordinary for a
small-town paper taking names down by ear.

## An earlier wife?

The Ancestry tree flags James's brothers and sisters as his *half*-siblings —
Walter A., Jessie Lee, Mary E., Frank P. and Bettie J.[^ancestry_tree_35916700]
If that is right, George had a wife before Harriet Eliza Sams who does not appear
in the tree at all, and this chart is missing a woman.

It should not be taken at face value. A half-sibling flag is what Ancestry shows
whenever children are attached to one parent and not the other, so it is at least
as likely to be a data-entry artifact as a fact. No name, date or record for an
earlier wife has been produced. It is recorded here as a question worth working,
not as a finding: the 1900 and 1910 censuses would settle it, since they record
how many children a woman had borne and how long a couple had been married.

One detail from the same batch does check out. Walter A. is given as dying 8
January 1955 at Corryton, Tennessee — just outside Knoxville, where his father's
1944 obituary placed him.[^ancestry_tree_35916700][^lewis_obit_1944_death] It
also explains why Walter is missing from James's own obituary six months later:
he was already dead.

## A census citation that cannot be right

The family's Ancestry tree attaches a 1860 census entry to him, placing him as an
infant at Luneys Creek in Hardy County.[^ancestry_tree_35916700] It is not his.
The 1860 census was enumerated from 1 June, and George was not born until that
December; Hardy County, now in West Virginia, is also some two hundred miles from
Washington County, where the rest of the family sits. The citation has been left
out rather than imported.

## The old family Bible

Notes found among the papers of Colleen Hoffman — Lorene Lewis's daughter — say
that George W. Lewis himself wrote several entries into a very old family
Bible.[^bible_notes_lewis] Of the loose handwritten transcription pages that
survive, some copy those entries and others are later family recollection and
summary in no known hand.[^bible_notes_lewis] So a few of the Bible readings may
trace back to George as a first-hand witness — his wife's name among them, set
down as "Harriet Boar" — while the rest carry no more weight than any family
story.

One page is wrong outright. It gives George's parents as "William W.S. (Samuel
William)" Lewis and "Elizabeth", and has a "William Lewis family" arriving in
Virginia from Charleston, South Carolina, in 1865 with George, born 1861, four
years old on the journey.[^bible_notes_lewis] None of it holds. His parents are
Philip Lewis and Margaret Rouse by the 1852 Ashe County marriage and the 1860
census,[^ashe_co_nc_marriage_1852][^census_1860_washington_lewis] which already
has Philip's family settled in Washington County, Virginia, before George's birth
that December — so there was no arrival from South Carolina in 1865. That sheet is
a summary in an unknown hand, not one of the entries traceable to George; it is
recorded only so the conflict is on file, and is not evidence for anything.
