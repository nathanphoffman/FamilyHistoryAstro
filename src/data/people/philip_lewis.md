## Biography

The Lewis name in this family runs back to Philip, and then it stops. He was born
around 1830 somewhere in North Carolina, married Margaret Rouse in the mountains
of Ashe County in the spring of 1852, and was dead before he reached his
mid-forties — leaving Margaret a young widow with children, who remarried within a
few years, because a widow in that place and time could not really afford not to.

Beyond those few dates, Philip is a closed door. No record names his parents; the
names that hang above him come only from other families' online trees. If the
Cherokee ancestry this family talks about is real, this is the branch where it
most plausibly belongs — the Lewis line reaching back into a corner of the
Carolina mountains long associated with mixed-ancestry communities — but "most
plausibly" is carrying a great deal of weight in that sentence, and nothing has
ever been proven.

He is, in short, the edge of the map on his side of the family: the last Lewis
anyone can actually see.

## Research Notes

Philip Lewis was born about 1830 in North Carolina and married Margaret Rouse at
Ashe County, North Carolina, on 11 March 1852.[^ashe_co_nc_marriage_1852] He died
young, somewhere between about 1867 and 1871, at roughly forty; Margaret
remarried Noah Kidd in 1871,[^marriage_1871_kidd_rouse] a quick remarriage of the
kind that economic necessity forced on a widow with children.

The Ancestry tree places that death in Pennsylvania, which would mean the move
north began a generation earlier than this chart has
it.[^ancestry_tree_35916700] No place of death is recorded here on that basis. It
sits awkwardly against everything else: his son George was still in Washington
County, Virginia, at the 1900 and 1910 censuses and only reached York County
later, so a Pennsylvania death in about 1870 would require the family to have
gone north, returned to Virginia for thirty years, and gone north again. Not
impossible — but it needs a record, not a tree.

His own parentage is unconfirmed. The family's Ancestry tree names Isaac Jacob
Lewis and Susannah Osborne as his parents,[^ancestry_tree_35916700] and a
FamilySearch profile proposes the same pair; neither attaches any
evidence.[^fs_tree_gf3k_p43] They are drawn into the chart marked unverified
rather than kept back as a hypothesis, but nothing beyond those two trees
connects them to Philip, and the generations above them are unverified
throughout.

A handwritten transcription of an old family Bible offers yet another version —
George's parents as "William W.S. Lewis" and "Elizabeth", the family arriving from
Charleston, South Carolina, in 1865.[^bible_notes_lewis] It is contradicted
outright by the 1852 marriage and the 1860 census and is set out under George's
record; it lends no weight here in any direction.

## The Native American question

If the family's claim of Cherokee ancestry is genuine, this is the branch that
best fits it — which is worth stating plainly alongside how little supports it.
Philip has been hypothetically linked to the Isaac Lewis and Susannah Osborne
family of Ashe County, a family sitting adjacent to the well-documented
"Sizemore" cluster — Lewis, Osborne, Blevins and Roark surnames — associated with
Melungeon ancestry. That cluster brought its own Cherokee claim in 1906, and
Guion Miller rejected it in Special Report 417 for want of documented
ancestry.[^guion_miller_417]

So the best-fit line runs to a family whose own claim failed on the evidence, by
a link that is itself unevidenced. Nothing here is established. The wider search
against every Cherokee roll returned no matches for any surname in this
tree.[^cherokee_roll_searches]
