## Biography

Margaret Surber was born in 1786 and died in 1847, and in the years between she
married a man named Phillip Rouse and raised a family, among them a son, Philip
Isaac Rouse, born in 1808. That is very nearly everything that can be said about
her directly.

Her importance to this family is structural. She is the hinge on which an enormous
branch swings — the Surbers back to Switzerland, the Hubbells back to colonial New
England, all of it hanging from her, and specifically from the unsettled question
of who her mother was. Her own birth year is the one fixed point in that argument:
she had a child in 1808, so she cannot have been born any later than the tree
already says, however inconvenient that is for the dates stacked above her.
Everything disputed in that part of the family gets measured against Margaret
Surber and the year 1786.

## Research Notes

Margaret Surber is given by the family's Ancestry tree as the wife of Phillip
Rouse.[^ancestry_tree_35916700] Through her the tree opens onto its longest and
most ornamented branch — the Surber line back to Zürich, and the Hubbell line
back into colonial Connecticut — none of which rests on a document.

Her own page in that tree names no father, though it names her mother's husband.
Adam Newell Surber is carried here on that reading.

## This is where the Hubbell descent is decided

Her mother is the more consequential problem. The tree dates Adam Newell Surber's
marriage to Margaret Hubbell to 1797, in Washington County, Virginia — eleven
years after this birth.[^ancestry_tree_35916700]

A daughter born eleven years before her parents' wedding is not impossible in
itself, but here it forces a choice, because Margaret Hubbell's own birth year is
under dispute for separate reasons. If that 1797 date is sound and Margaret
Hubbell was born as late as the tree's critics suggest, then this Margaret is
Adam's daughter by an earlier wife, and everything above her mother — the
Hubbells, the Bishops, the Nicholses, the Sanfords and the Germantown Surbers —
belongs to someone else's family, not to this one.

Her own dates are the fixed point in that argument. Her son Philip Isaac Rouse is
born in 1808, so her 1786 birth cannot be moved later to accommodate the
marriage; a birth after 1797 would make her a mother at ten. Whatever gives way
at this joint, it is not this record.

The 1797 Washington County marriage bond would settle it, and it sits in a free
collection. Until someone reads it, the link to Margaret Hubbell is the single
most load-bearing unverified claim on this chart.
