## Biography

At nineteen, Lorene Lewis was already living three hundred miles from home. She
had grown up in Glade Spring, a small railroad town in the Virginia mountains, the
daughter of James Hiram Lewis and Alice Stovall — but sometime before her
twentieth birthday she left on her own and turned up in York, Pennsylvania,
boarding in a stranger's house and waiting tables for a living. The rest of the
family would move north eventually; Lorene got there first, and alone.

In York she married Urie John Wallick, a local man who set type for a printing
company. The marriage stitched together two families with almost nothing in
common: hers reaching back into the hardscrabble farm country of southwest
Virginia, his rooted for generations in the Pennsylvania German households of York
County and the printing trades. They raised three children — Colleen, Barbara,
and a son, Urie Jim — moving between homes around York while Lorene kept house and
Urie worked at the press.

She spent the whole of her adult life in York and died there in the spring of
1994, at eighty-three, buried at Stewartstown in the county she had crossed the
mountains to reach as a girl. The old family Bible and the handwritten notes about
its Virginia beginnings have come down through her daughter Colleen.

## Research Notes

Lorene was born 20 March 1911 at Glade Spring, in Washington County, Virginia,
the daughter of James Hiram Lewis and Alice Louise Stovall.[^wash_co_va_marriage_1905]
Like her parents she moved north to York, Pennsylvania, where she spent the rest
of her life.

There she married Urie John Wallick, a York-born electrotyper. No marriage record
has been located, so neither the date nor the place is established. They had
three children: Colleen, born 30 July 1935; Barbara, born 1937 or 1938; and a
son, Urie Jim Wallick.

Her marriage is the join between two quite different families. Her own line runs
back into rural Washington County, Virginia — farm labour, wartime desertion,
poverty relief, and two generations of unrecorded fathers. Her husband's is
Pennsylvania German throughout, settled in York County for at least three
generations and working in the printing trades.

By 1930 she was living in York on her own account, aged nineteen, lodging in
someone else's house and working as a waitress — she had left Virginia alone
before she was twenty. This is as the family's Ancestry tree reports it; the
underlying 1930 census image has not been re-examined
here.[^census_1930_york][^ancestry_tree_35916700] The tree adds that the house
she lodged in was valued at $15,000, a detail typed in rather than cited, and an
inference from the census rather than a reading of
it.[^ancestry_tree_35916700] She appears in the 1940 census at New Salem, York
County, recorded as a wife — again as the tree reports it rather than as read
directly here.[^census_1940_new_salem]

By 1950 the household is at house 603 in York: Urie, 37, an electrotyper in a
printing company; Lorene, 39, keeping house, and recorded as born in Virginia —
the one place in this chart where her Virginia birth is written down in her own
household rather than inferred from where her parents were. With them are
Colleen, 14, Barbara, 12, and a son, James,
2.[^census_1950_york]

She died at York in May 1994 and is buried at Stewartstown, in York
County.[^ssdi_lorene_wallick][^findagrave_lorene] The year had previously been
open between 1993 and 1994; the Social Security Death Index settles it, though
the index entry itself has not been re-examined here.
