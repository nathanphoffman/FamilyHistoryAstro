## Biography

On the 1905 record of her daughter Alice's marriage, in the space meant for the
bride's parents, a clerk wrote a single word: *Maggie*. No father, not even an
initial. Nineteen years after Alice was born, Maggie Stovall was still the only
parent anyone could name.

Maggie had Alice in 1886, unmarried and in her early twenties, in a small
southwest Virginia community where that was a hard thing to be. Her own father had
walked out on the family years earlier, while she was still a teenager. She did
marry eventually — a man named William Roark, in 1894 — but that was eight years
after Alice's birth, and by 1910 she turns up on her own again, working as a
servant in Glade Spring. She lived until 1943.

Who Alice's father was, Maggie appears never to have told anyone who wrote it
down. It is the question her whole branch of the family still turns on.

## Research Notes

Maggie Stovall was born about 1862–64 to John Quincy Alexander Stovall and Mary
E. Lester, and died in 1943. Her father left the family around 1878–80, when she
was in her teens.[^jqa_stovall_absence]

Her daughter Alice was born in May 1886. Maggie did not marry until 16 August
1894, when she wed William Marshall Roark,[^wash_co_va_marriage_1894] so Roark
cannot be Alice's father, and Alice's paternity remains the open question in this
line.[^wash_co_va_bastardy]

The 1905 marriage register puts her at the centre of that question in the
clearest way available. In the column for the bride's parents it records one
name — "Maggie" — and no father at
all.[^wash_co_va_marriage_1905][^va_vital_index_1905] Nineteen years after
Alice's birth, Maggie was still the only parent anyone could name.

The 1910 census records her at Glade Spring as single and working as a servant,
sixteen years after her marriage.[^census_1910_glade_spring_maggie] Whether that
means she was widowed, separated, or simply recorded carelessly is not
established.

She is also where the family's Ancestry tree goes wrong. That tree carries her
twice — once as "Maggie Stovall" and once as "Maggie Roak", her name after the
1894 marriage — and then makes her father, John Quincy Alexander Stovall, into
the father of her daughter Alice as well.[^ancestry_tree_35916700] He was not:
he had left the area by about 1878–80, six years before Alice was
born.[^jqa_stovall_absence] That conflation has not been imported, and Alice's
father remains unrecorded.

Her surnames are worth keeping straight, since both have been read as evidence of
ancestry at one time or another. Stovall was hers from birth — an English name of
place-name origin. Roark, Irish and from Ó Ruairc, she acquired only by that 1894
marriage, eight years after Alice was born.
