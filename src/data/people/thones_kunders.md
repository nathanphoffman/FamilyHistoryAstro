## Biography

Thones Kunders is the most historically weighty figure this family tree so much as
brushes against — and one of the least securely attached. He was a real,
well-documented man: one of the thirteen Krefeld families who founded Germantown,
Pennsylvania, in 1683. His house hosted the settlement's first Quaker meetings,
and it was there, in 1688, that the Germantown protest against slavery — the first
document of its kind in the American colonies — was drawn up and signed.

The tree makes him the father of a "Henry Cunreds" on this line, treating the odd
surname as a worn-down spelling of Kunders. The history is rock solid; the
connection to this family is not, and a celebrated name sitting atop an
undocumented line is exactly the sort of thing that gets wished into place.
