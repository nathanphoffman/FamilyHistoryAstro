## Biography

By the end of his life, James Hiram Lewis was a Pennsylvania factory retiree
living on West King Street in the city of York — a long way, in miles and in kind,
from where he began. He was born in the far southwestern corner of Virginia
somewhere in the mid-1880s; the exact year was never pinned down, and the few
records that mention it don't agree. His parents were George Lewis and Harriet
Sams, and his mother handed him the middle name Hiram after her own father — a man
who had walked out on the family before she was grown.

At nineteen or twenty, working as a laborer, he married Alice Louise Stovall in
the autumn of 1905. What grew from that was a big family: ten children or more,
among them Lorene, Ella, Porter, Mae, Lucy, Samuel, Donald, Robert, and Dora
Jane, plus at least one daughter, Elizabeth Rosetta, who died young.

Somewhere in the working years the whole household pulled up and moved north to
York County. There James took a job at the York Naval Ordnance Plant — the only
line of work ever recorded for him — and stayed until he retired. He died at York
Hospital on a Saturday evening in July 1955 and was buried close by, at
Stewartstown.

## Research Notes

James Hiram Lewis was born, by the date carried here, on 12 December 1885. He
married Alice Louise Stovall on 18 October 1905 in Washington County, Virginia,
where the register records him as "Jas H Lewis", aged 20, a labourer, and gives
his parents in abbreviated form as "G Wm" and "Harriott" — readable as George
William Lewis and Harriet.[^wash_co_va_marriage_1905][^va_vital_index_1905] He
died 16 July 1955 and is buried at
Stewartstown Cemetery in York County, Pennsylvania,[^stewartstown_cemetery] the
family having moved north from Virginia at some point not yet established.

## The 1955 obituary

The York Daily Record of 18 July 1955 carried his obituary.[^lewis_obit_1955] It
gives his address as 540 West King Street, York; records that he died at 6 p.m.
on the Saturday — 16 July — at York Hospital; and describes him as a retired
employee of the York Naval Ordnance plant, the first occupation recorded for him
anywhere in this tree.

It names nine surviving children: Ella V. Kunkle of Fawn Grove, Porter B.,
Lorene E. Wallick, Mae Hovis of Lancaster, Lucy E., Samuel, Donald L., Robert
Lee of Baltimore, and Dora Jane. Only Lorene appears on this chart, which follows
the direct line alone. His siblings are given as Mary Wyingler, Maggie Freeman,
Betty Barbra and Frank Lewis — and "Betty Barbra" is the daughter at whose house
his father George died in 1944, whom that year's notice names as Mrs. Baldwin
Barbra.[^lewis_obit_1944_death]

An obituary lists the living, so it is not a list of his children. Two more are
known from elsewhere: Effie Mae Lewis Hovis, 1912–2004, who died at
Elizabethtown and is the "Mae Hovis" of the obituary under her full name; and
Elizabeth Rosetta Lewis, for whom a Virginia death certificate survives naming
her parents as Jas H Lewis and Allice Louise Stovall. Elizabeth Rosetta appears
in no obituary and no tree, which normally means a child who died before her
parents. Neither is charted here, since this tree follows the direct line only.

## When was he born?

The date carried here, 12 December 1885, is now contradicted by two independent
records, and it is worth setting them out plainly.

The obituary gives his age as 70. A birth on 12 December 1885 would make him 69
at his death in July 1955.[^lewis_obit_1955]

The 1905 marriage register gives his age as 20 on 18 October. A birth that
December would make him 19 — he would not turn 20 for another eight
weeks.[^va_vital_index_1905]

Both records push the same way, and each was taken down while the fact was
current rather than decades later. Together they point at a birth somewhere in
late 1884 or the first ten months of 1885. Against them stands the 12 December
1885 date itself, which entered this tree through an earlier research pass that
did not record which document it came from.[^prior_ai_research]

The date is kept, because two stated ages are not the same thing as a birth
record and both could have been rounded or misheard. But it is the weakest fact
on this record, and testing it has only made it messier: his WWI draft
registration, read directly, gives a third date — 17 January
1883[^wwi_draft_james_lewis] — agreeing with neither the 1885 date carried here
nor the late-1884/1885 window implied by the marriage register and obituary.
Three records now give three different years. The 1900 census, taken closest to
the event and naming him as a child in his parents' household, is probably the
best evidence available: it gives "James H.", age 15, born January
1885.[^census_1900_holston] That single record does not resolve the conflict on
its own, but it tips the weight of evidence toward the date already carried
here rather than away from it.

His earlier movements are traced through the census: the Holston District of
Washington County in 1900,[^census_1900_holston] and Glade Spring in
1920,[^census_1920_glade_spring] with a WWI draft registration between
them.[^wwi_draft_james_lewis]

The 1910 entry does not fit with them, and reading the image has now confirmed
why: it records a "J H Lewis" at Stony Creek in Sussex County — some 250 miles
east of every other fact about this family — with a wife Mary K. Lewis and son
Emitt P. Lewis, and FamilySearch's own tree attaches the record to a separate
person, James Herbert Lewis (1884–1936).[^census_1910_stony_creek] Different
middle name, different wife, different death year and place. The citation is
kept on file as a documented near-miss, not as evidence for this
James Hiram Lewis.
